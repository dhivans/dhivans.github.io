A checkmark next to a spec means DST has physically tested and confirmed it.

## How specs get tested

Dimensions and weight are measured directly with digital calipers and a scale. Electronics, such as flash size, chip identity, or GPIO count, are confirmed against the real hardware: reading chip markings directly, or using a tool like esptool. Anything else is checked by hand against the physical part, wherever that's actually possible.

## How it shows on a product page

A product with at least one tested spec gets a Tested by DST badge next to the Specs heading, and each tested spec has its own checkmark next to the value.

## If something looks wrong

We'd rather know than leave it wrong. Every Specs panel has a Spot a mistake link that opens a pre-filled email to us. Send it and we'll check it and fix it.

## Where the detail comes from

Manufacturers are asked directly for real specs and datasheets first. When that isn't available, some products have an open request on [Help Us Document the Catalogue](/help-document/), a UK-only programme where a product is sent out for free in exchange for the missing detail, evidenced with a photo.

Amazon handles checkout, fulfilment, and returns. DST's job is to make the product information clearer before you click through.
