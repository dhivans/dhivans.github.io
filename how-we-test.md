DST treats marketplace listings as claims, not proof. It's simple: a spec is either tested by DST, or it isn't — no tiers, no "manufacturer says" badge dressed up to look like a check.

## How specs actually get tested

- **Dimensions & weight** — measured directly with digital calipers and a scale.
- **Electronics** (flash size, chip identity, GPIO count, and similar) — confirmed against the real hardware, e.g. reading chip markings directly, or a tool like esptool.
- **Everything else** — checked by hand against the physical part wherever it's something you can actually measure or verify.

Most specs on a page are still just the manufacturer's or listing's word — that's normal, and shown as a plain fact like anything else. Nothing is marked as tested unless DST has actually done it.

## How it shows on a product page

- If DST has tested and confirmed at least one spec on a product, that page gets a **Tested by DST** badge next to the Specs heading.
- Any individual spec that's actually been tested gets its own blue checkmark next to the value.
- No badge, no checkmark, means exactly that — we haven't independently checked it yet, not that it's wrong.

## If something looks wrong

We'd rather know than have it sit there wrong. Every Specs panel has a **"Spot a mistake?"** link that opens a pre-filled email straight to us — no account, no form. If you spot anything off, send it and we'll check it and fix it.

## Where the detail comes from

Manufacturers are asked directly for real specs and datasheets first. Where that isn't available, some products have an open request on **[Help Us Document the Catalogue](/help-document/)** — a UK-only programme where a product is sent out for free in exchange for the specific missing detail, evidenced with a photo.

Amazon handles checkout, fulfilment, and returns. DST's job is to make the product information clearer before you click through.
